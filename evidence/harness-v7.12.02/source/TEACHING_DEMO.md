## Fractions

**Request:** Teach a beginner why 1/2 + 1/4 = 3/4. Use plain language and one worked example.

Core: Fractions count parts of a whole. The denominator names the part size; the numerator counts parts. Addition needs equal-sized pieces.

Example: Cut a pizza into four slices. Half is two slices: 1/2 = 2/4. Add one quarter: 2/4 + 1/4 = 3/4. You now have three of four slices.

Misconception: Do not add denominators. Once piece sizes match, add only the counts on top.

Check: What is 1/2 + 1/8, using eighths?

## Photosynthesis

**Request:** Explain photosynthesis to a middle-school learner. Distinguish energy from food.

Core: Photosynthesis uses light energy to turn carbon dioxide and water into sugar, releasing oxygen. Sugar stores chemical energy and provides material for growth.

Example: A watered bean plant by a sunny window can make sugar in its leaves when carbon dioxide is available. Light powers the chemical change; the water and carbon dioxide supply matter.

Misconception: Plants do not eat sunlight. Sunlight supplies energy, not food. Plants also use cellular respiration to release usable energy from sugar.

Check: Can light alone let a plant make sugar if carbon dioxide is missing?

## Independent Flips

**Request:** Teach a beginner why three heads do not make tails due on the next fair coin flip.

Core: For fair, independent flips, earlier results do not change the next probability. Fair means equal chances; independent means one result does not affect another.

Example: After heads, heads, heads, the next flip still has a 1/2, or 50%, chance of tails. The coin has no memory.

Misconception: Tails is not due. Expecting a streak to force an immediate opposite result is the gambler's fallacy. Independence is essential to this conclusion.

Check: After five tails, what is the chance of heads on the next fair, independent flip?

## Percentage Points

**Request:** Explain to a new analyst how an increase from 40% to 50% differs in percentage points and percent.

Core: Percentage points measure the difference between percentages. Percent change measures that difference relative to the starting value.

Example: From 40% to 50%, the difference is 10 percentage points. Relative increase: (50 - 40) / 40 * 100 = 25%. Divide by the original value, not the new rate.

Misconception: A rise of 10 percentage points is not necessarily a 10% increase. Relative percent change from zero is undefined because you cannot divide by zero.

Check: For a rise from 20% to 30%, what are both increases?
